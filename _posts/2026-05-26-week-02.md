This week was devoted primarily to an extensive literature review. 

I read research on machine-learning-based malware detection and classification, with particular attention to the ways researchers represent malicious behavior before applying classification algorithms.
One paper I examined used function-call graphs for IoT malware classification. This was useful because it emphasized that representation can be just as important as the classifier itself. Instead of treating malware as a collection of isolated features, graph-based methods preserve relationships among functions and behaviors.
I also reviewed more general work on machine learning for malware analysis and research involving IoT and Android malware. These papers provided background on static and dynamic malware-analysis techniques and helped me better understand the limitations of traditional signature-based approaches.
At this stage, my project was still intentionally broad. The main objective was to build enough background knowledge to identify a research question that was both meaningful and feasible.
