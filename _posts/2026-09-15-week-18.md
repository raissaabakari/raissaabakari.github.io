This week I looked beyond the aggregate scores to understand *why* the hybrid model behaved differently from the neural baseline.

Symbolic augmentation corrected **53 events that the neural model had classified incorrectly**, while it changed **23 previously correct neural predictions into incorrect predictions**. This produced a net gain of 30 correct test classifications.

Some corrected examples were intuitive:

- `exfiltrate → data` supported data exfiltration;
- `extract → information` supported data theft;
- `evade → detection` supported anti-detection;
- C&C-related domain references supported command and control.

The errors were equally informative. Data exfiltration was frequently over-predicted because communication-related features such as servers, HTTP, files, and sending information are also common in command-and-control activity.

The confusion matrix therefore became useful not just as an evaluation figure but as evidence of where simple symbolic rules struggle with overlapping cybersecurity concepts.
