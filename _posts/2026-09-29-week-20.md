The final week focused on completing the research report, preparing the project website, and reflecting on the overall DREAM experience.

The project changed substantially from its original scope. I began with the broad idea of **neuro-symbolic AI for malware analysis** and initially explored topics including retrieval-augmented generation, machine-learning malware classification, symbolic execution, and graph-based security analysis.

The final project became much more specific: testing whether structured malware-behavior knowledge could complement neural semantic representations when classifying behavior described in threat-intelligence reports.

One of my biggest takeaways from the project is that narrowing the research question is itself a major part of the research process. Many of the early ideas were interesting but far too broad to evaluate meaningfully within one project.

The experiment ultimately produced a working proof-of-concept system, a comparative evaluation of neural, symbolic, and hybrid methods, and several directions for future research. It also gave me experience with literature review, dataset preprocessing, machine-learning experimentation, error analysis, technical writing, and communicating research results.
