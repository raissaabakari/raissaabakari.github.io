After several weeks of reading, I began narrowing the project from the broad idea of “neuro-symbolic AI for malware analysis” into a testable research question.

One issue was the practical complexity of working directly with malicious binaries. A binary-analysis project would require appropriate malware samples, safe execution infrastructure, sandboxing, and substantially more implementation work.

Threat-intelligence reports offered an alternative. They contain detailed descriptions of malware behavior without requiring the experiment itself to execute malicious software.

This led to a more focused direction: studying **malware behavior described in threat-intelligence text**.

The emerging research question became whether explicit, structured malware knowledge could improve a neural model's ability to categorize those behaviors. This also suggested a natural experimental design involving three systems: a neural baseline, a symbolic baseline, and a combined neuro-symbolic system.
