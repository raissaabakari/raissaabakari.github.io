The next challenge was defining the classification task.

MalwareTextDB contains 20 different malware capability categories, but the frequency of these labels varies considerably. Including every category would have produced a much more difficult classification problem with several sparsely represented classes.

For the proof-of-concept experiment, I selected six capabilities with enough examples to support evaluation:

- command and control;
- infection/propagation;
- machine access/control;
- anti-detection;
- data theft;
- data exfiltration.

After retaining single-label examples belonging to these classes, the final experimental dataset contained **1,796 malware-behavior events**.

This also introduced a class-imbalance issue, since command-and-control and infection/propagation were considerably larger than the other classes. Because of that imbalance, I decided that macro-F1 would be important alongside overall accuracy.
