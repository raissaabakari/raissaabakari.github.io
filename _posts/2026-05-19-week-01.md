My first week of DREAM research focused on defining the broad direction of the project. The starting topic was neuro-symbolic artificial intelligence for malware analysis, which brought together several areas I was interested in exploring: cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, and structured reasoning.
Because malware analysis covers a wide range of tasks, one of the first challenges was determining what aspect of the problem could reasonably be studied within the scope of the apprenticeship. Malware analysis can involve static binary analysis, dynamic execution, reverse engineering, malware-family classification, threat intelligence, and many other approaches.
I began by discussing the project direction with my research advisor and identifying literature that could help clarify the potential scope. Rather than beginning implementation immediately, the initial goal was to understand how neural and symbolic approaches were already being applied in cybersecurity and where there might be room for a manageable proof-of-concept study.
