My literature review continued this week, with more attention to **symbolic and graph-based malware analysis**.

I reviewed research using symbolic execution, including approaches that explore multiple possible program paths and construct structured representations of malware behavior. I also studied SEMA, a symbolic-execution toolchain that extracts API and system-call information and converts it into graph representations for later analysis.

An important realization this week was that the word *symbolic* can mean different things depending on the research context. Formal symbolic execution of binary programs is substantially different from the kind of symbolic representation I was considering for my own work.

This distinction became important to the project. My experiment would not perform symbolic execution of malware binaries. Instead, I became interested in whether explicit representations of malware actions and relationships could be combined with neural representations of threat-intelligence text.
